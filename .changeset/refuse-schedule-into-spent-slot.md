`scheduleExecution` refuses a schedule into a slot that has already been broadcast: it throws `cannot proceed. the slot (chainId: ..., account: ..., slot: ...) has already been used: ...`, and `@fuzd/server`'s `/api/scheduling/scheduleExecution` answers HTTP 500 with `{success: false, errors: [{message}]}`. A slot is spent once its queued record is marked broadcasted or the executor holds a record for any of its batch indices (so a partially broadcast batch spends it too). Before, the schedule answered `success: true` and was never sent: the executor found the earlier batch's records and returned them with `slotAlreadyUsed` without sending, and only the indices beyond the earlier batch's length were broadcast. A slot that is only queued is still replaced by a new schedule, and a slot whose schedule was archived before anything was broadcast is free again. Schedule into a new slot instead. A schedule into a slot that a processing run holds right now is refused too (`cannot proceed. the slot (...) is being processed: its execution may be broadcasting right now. try again in a moment.`): until the executor has stored what it sends, such a slot looks free, and a schedule accepted then would never be sent. A direct broadcast (`/api/execution/broadcastExecution`) into a slot that holds a queued schedule is refused as well (`cannot proceed. the slot (...) has a queued schedule ...`), unless it is the schedule's own transaction (a one-transaction schedule in clear): that is sent now, and the schedule finds it sent. And a schedule that finds, when it runs, that another execution took its slot after it was accepted (a direct broadcast in flight when it was scheduled) is archived with that as its `lastError`, instead of being marked broadcast without being sent; none of its later batch indices is sent then (the error says how many were).

Storage interface: `SchedulerStorage.queueExecution(execution, {now})` stores a new schedule in one atomic upsert, unless the slot's queued execution is broadcast or held by a run; `RemoteSQLSchedulerStorage` implements it, and `scheduleExecution` writes with it. `@fuzd/executor`: `broadcastExecution` answers `slotAlreadyUsed` also when it finds the slot used once it holds the broadcaster lock (it returned the record unflagged). `@fuzd/common` exports `sameTransaction(a, b)`.
